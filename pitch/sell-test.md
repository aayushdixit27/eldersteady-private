# Sell test

The lock opens only if all five conditions are true. Today, two are supported by a coherent proposition and three still need buyer evidence or shipped proof.

## 1. They love the product — not yet validated

The proposition is: **know when help may be needed without gaining the ability to watch.** No wearable, parent app, call centre, stored clip, or cloud account. That is emotionally sharper than another fall detector, but no target buyer has yet seen this prototype and said they want it.

Remaining work: conduct five observed conversations with adult children who coordinate care and five with older adults; record objections and willingness to trial without turning testimony into frequency.

## 2. They trust the seller — not yet earned

Trust starts with falsifiable boundaries: no frames stored or transmitted, no face recognition, no audio, no diagnosis, no 911 claim. The fixed eval demands exact ledger arithmetic and board-local execution with the Mac link down.

Remaining work: pass all six evals in public, show the unplugged board process, publish retention and failure behaviour, and have an independent reviewer reproduce the ledger sum.

## 3. They believe *they* can get the result — plausible, not yet validated

Lead with the buyer’s new capability: a daughter can respond to an urgent night episode without installing an app on her father’s phone or receiving a video of him. A parent can accept help without accepting remote observation.

The first-use path is intentionally ordinary:

1. Choose up to three rooms and trusted family contacts.
2. A local installer positions cameras; the parent touches nothing.
3. Stage one fall, one night route, and one stove check.
4. Agree who receives urgent versus review-only notices.
5. Run a two-week calibration period in which low-confidence events remain reviewable.
6. Read the privacy ledger: incidents and exact destroyed-frame counts, never clips.

This makes adoption imaginable, but it does not yet prove installation success, alert delivery, accessibility, or that a parent accepts the device after living with it.

Remaining work: complete a supervised installation in a representative home; have the family explain the response plan back in their own words; measure false-alert burden during a consented trial.

## 4. There is a clear game plan — specified, not yet operational

After “yes,” the buyer receives:

- Day 0: consent and room-boundary review; name one primary and one backup contact.
- Day 1: local installation and the three staged checks.
- Days 2–14: calibration; urgent alerts go to the primary contact, while ambiguous observations stay findable for daily review.
- Day 14: tune thresholds and escalation timing from the family’s observed burden.
- Day 30: review response time, unwanted alerts, missed staged events, and the exact privacy ledger; continue or remove the system.

Success means the family correctly receives one actionable incident per staged scenario, can find every suppressed low-confidence observation, and sees exact frame accounting. Removal means unplugging and taking away the local unit; there is no cloud account or video archive to clean up.

Remaining work: implement contact delivery, configuration, calibration review, audit export, and removal instructions. Name an accountable operator for install and support.

## 5. There is true urgency — contextual, not universal

Named horizon: 30 days. If the family acts, it can establish and rehearse an escalation plan while verifying that images stay local. If it waits, privacy remains intact, but the awareness gap remains: a fall, night-wandering episode, or stove risk may be discovered by a call, neighbour, or later visit. The problem does not become a quantified catastrophe; we do not have a rate.

Urgency exists when a family is already seeing warning signs or rejecting surveillance products. It should not be manufactured for families without that need.

Remaining work: identify the buyer’s actual triggering event and decision date in discovery. Do not substitute search-indexed anecdotes for measured prevalence.

## Limits

No buyer interviews, field trial, pricing test, installation test, alert-delivery proof, or independent privacy audit is present in this worktree. Problem testimony may establish that surveillance concerns exist, but it cannot establish frequency. Four-of-five is insufficient; this sell test does not pass today.
